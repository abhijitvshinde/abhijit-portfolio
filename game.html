What I Do Next?

Meet ROV-7, an underwater robot connected to a ship by a zipline data cable. ROV-7's job is to prevent, detect, and mitigate an oil leak — but it can only do what you tell it. Type an instruction for what ROV-7 should do next. If your answer is too general, ROV-7 will ask "How?" until you give it a real plan. Stuck? Keep trying — ROV-7 starts dropping hints after a few attempts.

        Wrap-Up

          For Parents & Teachers

          This game was designed for students in grades 5–8 to build intuition about how real inspection robots handle infrastructure problems: checking similar spots proactively (prevention), using the right sensor for the right question (detection), and responding in the correct order — stop the source, contain the spill, then report it (mitigation). Answers are evaluated loosely on keywords, so encourage kids to type full, specific instructions rather than single words.

          FIRST LEGO League Challenge · Ages 9–11

          FLL Attachment Lab

          Build attachments for your SPIKE Prime robot in 3D: snap LEGO beams, axles, panels and a motor onto the robot, then test them on the BIOGLOW missions. When it works, build it for real!

          For Parents & Coaches

          In the Attachment Lab, students build attachments from Technic-style pieces on a 3D SPIKE Prime robot and test them against simplified models of the 15 BIOGLOW missions. The test checks reach, height, alignment and motor movement, and tells kids what to change ("2 studs higher", "turn the motor the other way") without giving a finished solution. Students log in with accounts their coach creates, and coaches can open every student's 3D build and test results from the coach dashboard. The mission models are simplified, so always try the real attachment on the real field. The official FIRST® LEGO® League Robot Game Rulebook and Challenge Updates are always the final authority.
